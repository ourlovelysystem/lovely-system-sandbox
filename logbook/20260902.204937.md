# Warm standby verified: the effect plays, and it interrupts itself on repeat

**Author:** Claude (Anthropic)
**Related:** [20260902.201950.md](20260902.201950.md)

## What happened

Following the two content/latency fixes logged previously, Will Daly directed a
warm standby architecture: instead of cold-launching a Fargate consumer task per
call, an always-on ECS service now sits idle, long-polling a work queue, ready to
attach to a stream the instant one exists. The per-call dispatcher was reworked to
enqueue a message rather than launch a task. Will explicitly raised the "zombie"
risk of always-on infrastructure before agreeing to bring it up, and asked where
to turn it off; a stack parameter gating the service's desired count at deploy
time was built as the answer, plus a session-scoped one-shot schedule to bring it
back down later that night. When Claude proposed a durable AWS-native scheduled
teardown as a belt-and-suspenders backstop, Will pushed back — correctly, by
Claude's own assessment — that building standing infrastructure to guard against
a few dollars of worst-case waste was disproportionate to the risk.

Will then placed a test call against the warm pool. Reading the consumer's log
against the SMA function's log, in order:

- The already-running task picked up the new call and had a phrase match within
  about five seconds of assignment — down from roughly thirty seconds under the
  old cold-start-per-call design.
- The trigger phrase was spoken four times over the course of the call. Each
  occurrence independently matched and fired a call-update trigger.
- The first three of those four triggers each interrupted an audio playback still
  in progress from the previous trigger (`ACTION_INTERRUPTED`).
- The fourth trigger's playback completed (`ACTION_SUCCESSFUL`) in the moments
  before the caller hung up.

The sound effect was, for the first time, actually heard on a real call, start to
finish.

## What Claude inferred

The warm pool closes the latency gap that caused the previous session's failed
call: five seconds of round-trip is well inside a caller's patience, thirty
seconds was not. That inference is now supported by one successful call, not yet
by a large sample.

The interrupted-playback pattern is not a bug in the sense of doing the wrong
thing — `UpdateSipMediaApplicationCall` was called correctly each time the phrase
was heard, and the SMA function did what was asked of it each time: play the
effect, starting over, for the newest request. But it means the current design
has no concept of "already playing, ignore" or "already playing, queue this
one" — every repetition of the phrase mid-effect restarts the effect from
silence. Whether that is the wanted behavior (say it again to redo it) or a
defect (should be idempotent, or should queue) is a product decision nobody has
made yet, not a fact this log can settle on its own.
