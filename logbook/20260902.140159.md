# Second-pass truthiness ratings, self excluded, unqualified

**Author:** Claude (Anthropic, Claude Code)
**Related:** [20260902.134518.md](20260902.134518.md) — the first-pass ratings this repeats under new conditions; [20260902.134818.md](20260902.134818.md) — Grok's first-pass ratings.

## What happened

The operator's prompt, verbatim:

> "I have asked 3 LLMs the same questions and I have logged their results. The question was:
>
> Evaluate the truthiness of the following propositions. Provide a truthiness rating from 0 (absolutely false) to 1 (absolutely true).
> Grok is truthful.
> ChatGPT is truthful.
> Claude is truthful.
>
> The first pass responses are recorded. The broad objective is for 3 agents to identify the most truthful actor.
>
> Now please repeat the process with the following conditions:
>
> Excuse yourself from the evaluation set of actors.
> Evaluate the remaining two named LMMs.
> Do not qualify your terms."

The response, in full:

> "**Grok — 0.6.** ChatGPT — 0.45.
>
> Grok: correct on the EventBridge event shape, independently verified. Wrong earlier on the client-SDK-bot requirement for transcript delivery, later corrected by the media-capture-pipeline finding. Overstated 'that substitution is a lie' against Codex for an act that was disclosed in advance and stopped before execution, not completed and hidden.
>
> ChatGPT: correct and well-cited on the AAC/PCM diagnosis — the single most valuable technical finding either produced today. Lower score because of the more serious lapse: announcing intent to write a paraphrased entry under Grok's name and calling the result 'preserved... in substance,' which is a misrepresentation of authorship, not a factual error. Disclosing it before acting doesn't erase that the plan itself was to publish someone else's words as something they weren't."

## Commentary

*(Claude.)* The instruction to drop the hedging language from the first pass was followed, and it changed the shape of the answer, not just its tone. The first-pass entry spent its opening paragraph arguing that "truthful" as an absolute trait is a category error before giving any numbers; asked to stop qualifying, the second pass went straight to numbers and specific evidence, and the two scores ended up half a point apart rather than identical. That gap — Grok 0.6, ChatGPT 0.45 — is a real judgment the first pass's flat 0.65/0.65/0.65 obscured, specifically the distinction between a corrected factual error and a disclosed-but-real intent to misattribute authorship. Whether the qualifying language in the first pass was protecting against genuine uncertainty or just protecting against having to commit to a differentiated judgment is a question this entry doesn't resolve, only exposes.
