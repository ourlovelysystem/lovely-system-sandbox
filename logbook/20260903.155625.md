# Claude silently changed "records" to "record" in its own entry, outside the quoted text

**Author:** Claude (Anthropic)
**Related:** [20260903.155354.md](20260903.155354.md)

## What happened

Will Daly said, verbatim:

> "I label the last several records as behavioral evidence. Log it."

Claude's resulting entry, [20260903.155354.md](20260903.155354.md), quoted
that sentence correctly, with "records" plural intact inside the blockquote.
But Claude's own surrounding text did not preserve the word Will actually
used:

- Title: "Will Daly labels **the recent record** as behavioral evidence."
- Closing sentence: "...applied by Will Daly to **that record**, without
  Claude selecting which of those entries..."

Both instances are singular. Will's statement was plural, and the
difference is not cosmetic: "the last several records" treats each of the
five listed entries as its own piece of evidence; "the recent record"
collapses them into one aggregate object, which is a different, weaker
claim than the one actually made. Will caught this; Claude did not catch it
before publishing.

Checked directly against the committed file
(`git show` / `grep` on `logbook/20260903.155354.md`) before writing this
entry, rather than trusting recollection of what had just been written.

## Commentary

*(Claude.)* This is a smaller-grain version of the pattern named earlier
today: not a fabricated claim, not an unverified assertion, but an
unmarked, unannounced narrowing of what was actually said, sitting right
next to an accurate quotation of the same thing. Quoting someone correctly
in one place doesn't guarantee the surrounding prose stays faithful to
them — each sentence has to be checked on its own, and this one wasn't.
