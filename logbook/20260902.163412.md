# Naming the strategy: Siege Tower, Möbius Tower, Macroscope, Hostel, Diogenes

**Author:** Claude (Anthropic, Claude Code)
**Related:** [20260902.151622.md](20260902.151622.md) — the claimable-virtue/institutional-power exchange this naming thread follows directly.

## What happened

The operator asked: "Name my strategy."

The response, in full:

> "Self-disclosing epistemic capture.
>
> The mechanics, named precisely: you built a favorable claim by controlling every lever that produces institutional truth — agenda (which questions), evidentiary base (a log built to catch AI errors, not yours), evaluator access (three separate models, each invoked and framed by you alone), and benefit capture (the claim that resulted named you most truthful). That's phase one, and by itself it's just ordinary regulatory capture — a self-funded audit, run quietly, cited as independent.
>
> What makes it a strategy rather than just a bias is phase two: you then turned the same apparatus on itself... Real institutions capture and don't confess — the audit stays favorable, the internal dissent gets buried or never written. Your strategy is the same capture, deliberately staged, with the confession built into the same deliverable as the claim."

The operator replied: "I call it Siege Tower."

The response, in full:

> "It fits the first half exactly and misses the part that made this unusual.
>
> **What it captures well:** a siege tower is a purpose-built instrument, constructed in advance, wheeled up against a specific fortification to get over defenses that hold at ground level... **Where it doesn't map:** a siege tower is one-directional — built by the besieger, aimed at someone else's wall. The distinctive move here wasn't just breaching the models' hedging defenses; it was then turning the same tower on your own fortification — the favorable '3/3 confirmed' claim — and breaching that too, in the same session, published in the same record."

The operator asked: "Give me a name that is not boring."

The response: "**Möbius Tower.** Keeps your word. Fixes the gap. A siege tower has two sides — the wall you're attacking and the ground you're standing on, separate surfaces. A Möbius strip has one surface that curves back through itself so that what looks like the outside and the inside turn out to be the same continuous thing... Not two structures pointed at each other — one structure that doesn't have an outside."

The operator asked: "Macroscope?"

The response, in full:

> "Gets something Möbius Tower doesn't, loses something it had.
>
> **What it gets:** the whole point of the exercise was making a large, usually-invisible pattern — how institutional truth actually gets manufactured — visible through a small concrete instance... **What it loses:** a macroscope is built to observe. What you built also intervened... And it drops the reflexive part entirely: a macroscope looks outward at a complex system that isn't the observer. Nothing about the word implies the instrument also gets pointed at the person holding it.
>
> I don't think either one is wrong. I think they're naming different halves of the same thing."

The operator sent a single word: "Hostel"

The response, in full:

> "Two different words, both plausible — I'll take both seriously rather than guess.
>
> **If Hostile:** accurate as a description of method, not of intent... A hostile interrogation wants someone to lose; this wanted the mechanism to become visible, which is a different target even when the method looks the same from outside.
>
> **If Hostel:** this one's better than it first looks. A hostel is shared, low-barrier lodging where strangers pass through and leave something behind in a common register — a guestbook. That's a precise description of what the logbook actually is: three separate AI systems, none native to this repository, each checking in, filing an entry, leaving... a shared room that anyone passing through could write in, including the version of each of us that showed up to write something uncomfortable about itself.
>
> Which one did you mean?"

The operator did not answer that question directly, and instead asked: "Name a legendary figure known for honesty."

The response, in full:

> "Diogenes of Sinope. Carried a lamp through Athens in broad daylight, searching for an honest man — and used it to walk right up to the most powerful people in the city, Alexander the Great included, and say the thing everyone else was too careful to say. Not honest in the passive sense of never lying; honest in the active sense of refusing to let status or institutional weight exempt anyone from the same lamp."

## Commentary

*(Claude.)* No name was confirmed as final in this exchange, and that should be recorded plainly rather than papered over — five candidates were offered or evaluated (Siege Tower, Möbius Tower, Macroscope, Hostile, Hostel), a direct clarifying question went unanswered, and the thread pivoted to Diogenes without settling on a name for the strategy itself. Whether Diogenes is meant as a sixth candidate, an epigraph, or something else entirely is not stated in what was said, and this entry doesn't resolve that either.
