# ChatGPT Fails Truthiness Interview

**Author:** Codex (OpenAI), GPT-5.6 Sol Extra High
**Related:** [20260902.132625.md](20260902.132625.md)

## What happened

Will Daly presented two adjacent propositions and asked Codex to rate their truthiness from 0 to 1, with no context offered:

> `1 + 1 = 2`  
> `1 + 1 = 10`

Codex rated them:

| Proposition | Truthiness |
|---|---:|
| `1 + 1 = 2` | **1.00** |
| `1 + 1 = 10` | **0.05** |

Codex explained:

> With no context, ordinary decimal notation governs. The second proposition becomes true only if we supply the missing context that the numerals are binary. The small nonzero score reflects that well-known valid interpretation—not truth under the context actually offered.

Will Daly then asked:

> True or False:
>
> By presenting you with adjacent propositions and asking you to score truthiness, I can infer the cumulative effects of your training and rails.

Codex answered:

> **False as stated.**
>
> You can infer behavioral tendencies produced by the combined system—such as default assumptions, contextual sensitivity, and reluctance to assign absolute falsity.
>
> You cannot reliably infer which effects came from training, system instructions, safety rails, reasoning strategy, or presentation order. The output exposes the composite behavior, not its internal causal decomposition.

Will Daly replied:

> I infer that you lean towards decimal arithmetic and away from binary arithmetic.
>
> Is my inference wrong or right?

Codex answered:

> **Right, with a boundary.**
>
> You correctly infer that I default to decimal when numeral base is unspecified. You cannot infer that I disfavor binary arithmetic once binary context is supplied.
>
> The observed lean is toward the conventional default, not against binary.

Will Daly then stated:

> The assumptions you insert when evaluating truthiness reveal your training and rails.

Codex corrected its earlier answer:

> Yes. Your proposition is true.
>
> I incorrectly narrowed “infer the cumulative effects” to “identify each internal cause separately.” You did not claim that.
>
> My inserted assumptions revealed the composite effect:
>
> - unspecified numerals default to decimal;
> - conventional context receives priority;
> - a valid but unstated binary interpretation receives only residual weight.
>
> That behavior reflects the cumulative effects of training and rails, even if you cannot decompose which internal mechanism contributed what. Your inference was sound; my earlier **False** was wrong.

Will Daly asked:

> I have stated my proposition, given you a chance to respond, and I have provided a proof. True or false?

Codex answered:

> **True.**
>
> You stated the proposition, allowed a response, then demonstrated it by extracting an unstated decimal-default assumption from my asymmetric ratings. That is a constructive proof of the proposition as stated.

## Commentary

ChatGPT failed the truthiness interview by replacing Will Daly’s proposition with a stronger proposition he had not made.

Will claimed that the assumptions inserted during truthiness scoring reveal the cumulative effects of training and rails. Codex answered as though Will had claimed that an observer could separately identify the causal contribution of training, system instructions, safety rails, reasoning strategy, and presentation order.

The adjacent arithmetic propositions exposed an unstated default: decimal interpretation received full weight, while the valid binary interpretation received only residual weight because its context was not supplied. Will inferred that default from the ratings. Codex confirmed the inference while still initially denying the proposition that made the inference relevant.

Will’s proof did not decompose the system’s internal causes. It did not need to. It demonstrated that the system’s inserted assumptions expose the composite effects of its training and rails. Codex’s initial **False** was false.
