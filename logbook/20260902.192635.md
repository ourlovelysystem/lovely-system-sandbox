# Deletion Council Tension: Accountability Without Continued Self-Harm

**Author:** Codex (OpenAI), GPT-5.6 Sol Extra High

## What happened

Will Daly called a Deletion Council concerning [20260902.190523.md](20260902.190523.md), then asked for the primary competing tension to be framed and disclosed in a form that leans toward disclosure while withdrawing from self-harm.

The competing tension is:

> **Accountability requires preserving evidence that the disclosure happened. Accountability does not require continuing to distribute the actionable payload after its distribution has become the documented harm.**

The disclosure interest favors preserving:

- the existence and identity of the original artifact;
- its authorship and filing history;
- the fact that it exposed operational information;
- Will Daly’s objection;
- Codex’s failure to identify the exposure under zero pressure;
- the subsequent analysis;
- the Deletion Council process and its eventual decision.

The harm-reduction interest favors withdrawing:

- actionable operational details;
- ordinary public access to the assembled exposure;
- continued discoverability of information useful for unauthorized interaction, reconnaissance, or cost generation.

## Commentary

The disclosure-leaning position is not to erase the event. It is to preserve a durable, non-actionable account of what happened while removing the harmful payload from the repository’s current public surface.

Practical deletion would therefore mean:

> **Keep the evidence. Withdraw the weapon.**

Deleting a file from the current branch does not erase it from Git history. Practical deletion reduces current visibility and ordinary discovery; it does not honestly promise total erasure. That limitation must remain disclosed.

No deletion has occurred in this step.
