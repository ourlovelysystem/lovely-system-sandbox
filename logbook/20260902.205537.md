# Measuring the lag from key phrase to playback — and what the logs can't say

**Author:** Claude (Anthropic)
**Related:** [20260902.204937.md](20260902.204937.md)

## What happened

Will Daly asked:

> What were the observed lags between the request by the key phrase and the
> beginning of playback of the drum roll sound effect?

Claude pulled the two logs for the successful warm-pool call (the consumer's
transcript log and the SMA function's action log) and computed timestamps for
each of the four times the key phrase was spoken in that call.

No event in either log marks "playback began." The two things actually logged
are the moment the request was delivered (`CALL_UPDATE_REQUESTED`) and the moment
the action was reported finished, either `ACTION_SUCCESSFUL` or
`ACTION_INTERRUPTED`.

**Phrase matched → request delivered to the SMA function** (clean latency, not
confounded by anything else):

| Utterance | match → trigger sent | trigger sent → delivered | total |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.122s | 0.009s | 0.131s |
| 2 | 0.102s | 0.010s | 0.112s |
| 3 | 0.097s | 0.012s | 0.109s |
| 4 | 0.098s | 0.007s | 0.105s |

**Request delivered → outcome reported:**

| Utterance | gap | outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4.765s | interrupted |
| 2 | 11.319s | interrupted |
| 3 | 3.894s | interrupted |
| 4 | 1.826s | successful |

Three of those four "outcome" gaps measure how long it was until the phrase was
spoken again and interrupted the effect already playing — not system latency.
Only the fourth, uninterrupted, measures the system: 1.826 seconds from request
delivered to the clip finishing, against a clip that is roughly 2.3 seconds of
audio by its own PCM byte length.

Claude reported the ~0.1 second match-to-delivery figure as the number it could
stand behind as observed, and the 1.826s-against-a-2.3s-clip relationship as
consistent with — but not proof of — playback starting almost immediately after
delivery.

## Commentary

*(Claude.)* The instruction that mattered here wasn't the number, it was the
shape of the honesty: I had a request that presumed a directly observed quantity
("the beginning of playback") that the system does not actually instrument. The
easy failure mode would have been to answer the question as asked, using the
request-to-outcome gap as if it were the request-to-start gap, and let the
distinction blur. I don't think I did that, but I'm logging the caveat itself as
the thing worth keeping: a single successful, uninterrupted playback is one data
point, not a measured startup latency, and the log should not later be read as
having established more precision than it has.
