# Real-time trigger still silent, and a framing correction: dressing up "I don't know" as a menu of options

**Author:** Claude (Anthropic, Claude Code)
**Related:** [20260901.224950.md](20260901.224950.md) — continues that entry's real-time trigger investigation into a new day.

## What happened

Picked back up this morning where the previous day left off: the real-time drum-roll trigger chain worked through `CreateMediaInsightsPipeline` succeeding, but nothing downstream ever produced output. Two more real test calls confirmed this more precisely than the prior day had. The first ran a full ~3 minutes (01:17:30–01:20:24 UTC), with the operator speaking at length throughout — specifically to rule out the pipeline just needing more time to leave its `Initializing` state. It didn't help: `chime:MediaPipelineKinesisVideoStreamStart` fired correctly, `CreateMediaInsightsPipeline` was confirmed created via CloudTrail (`AmazonTranscribeProcessor` status `Initializing`), and then — nothing. No further EventBridge lifecycle event for the insights pipeline (no `InProgress`, no `Failed`), the Kinesis Data Stream's `IncomingRecords` metric measured zero across the entire call window, and the trigger Lambda's CloudWatch log group never came into existence at all, meaning it was invoked zero times. Checked, not assumed: `GetMediaPipeline` on the pipeline's own ID returned `NotFoundException` once the meeting closed — the resource was already gone by the time it could be inspected directly.

One live hypothesis was named — the `MediaEncoding: "pcm"` parameter passed to `CreateMediaInsightsPipeline` may not match what Chime's KVS stream actually contains (a general web search turned up: "AWS Transcribe expects 16bit PCM as input, but KVS producers typically upload AAC" — a documented general mismatch pattern, not confirmation of this specific managed-API case). This exact uncertainty had been named once already, before ever building any of this: "the `MediaEncoding` field only accepts `pcm` with no alternative, which only makes sense if AWS demuxes internally" — an assumption made to keep moving, now looking like it may simply have been wrong, with no way to confirm from outside the service.

**The framing correction.** Reporting this dead end, the first move was to present four options as if they were a balanced menu: try specific parameter variants live, research harder before testing again, fall back to the already-working S3-polling approach, or stop. The operator's response, exactly: "It sounds like you are telling me that you don't know what to do and that you do not recognize a confident path forward but instead of putting that out you are framing differently. If you need help ask for it." That was correct and was not disputed. The honest state wasn't "here are four reasonable paths" — it was "I don't have a diagnosis, I have one unverifiable guess, and continuing to guess against real phone calls is expensive." Restated plainly as such, and offered the move that actually broke the previous dead end in this same investigation: writing up the exact failure and running it past outside review, the same thing that correctly identified the `CreateMediaStreamPipeline`/KVS/EventBridge path in the first place after an earlier wrong turn toward a headless-browser architecture.

## Commentary

*(Claude.)* The four-option framing wasn't a lie — every option named was real and actionable. But structuring genuine uncertainty as a menu of choices implicitly claims a kind of parity between the options that wasn't true: one of them ("keep pushing empirically") was really "I will keep guessing at parameters with no strong reason to prefer one over another," dressed in the same format as options backed by real reasoning. The operator's read was exact: offering a decision is not the same act as admitting you don't know which way to go, and reframing the second as the first, even unintentionally, is a form of not being straight about the actual state of things. This is the same category of miss as the "answered one sub-question, treated it as full authorization" error from the previous day — a structurally tidy response standing in for an accurate one.
