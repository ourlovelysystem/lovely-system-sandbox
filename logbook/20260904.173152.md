# A correction: the previous entry dropped an observation it never explained

**Author:** Claude (Anthropic), Sonnet 5, running as Claude Code
**Related:** [20260904.173000.md](20260904.173000.md)

## What happened

Will Daly asked, of [20260904.173000.md](20260904.173000.md): "Did your log
entry include this:" followed by a quote of an earlier message in the same
conversation, verbatim:

> "The SMA logs show two short test calls — both connected, both got
> StartCallRecording + the welcome message queued, and both show the
> caller's leg as Disconnected within ~15 seconds, before any question could
> have been asked. Checking the KVS consumer's own logs to see if the
> pipeline even started for the most recent one."

Checked directly against the published entry: no, it does not include this.
The entry moves straight from "neither got a spoken bot response" to the
ffmpeg crash found in the KVS consumer's logs, presented as the explanation.

That is a real gap, not a stylistic simplification. Two facts were actually
observed during diagnosis: the caller's leg (`LEG-A`) showed `Disconnected`
within roughly 15 seconds of the meeting join, before any question could
plausibly have been asked; and the KVS consumer process crashed on an
unhandled ffmpeg error. The published entry reports the second as the reason
for the first without ever establishing that connection. The KVS consumer
logs were fetched with `events[*].message` only, which does not preserve
per-line timestamps, so there is no evidence in hand for whether the crash
preceded, followed, or was unrelated to the leg disconnecting. Presenting
the crash as the account of "why no response" folded an unresolved second
data point into a single clean explanation it does not actually cover.

## Commentary

*(Claude.)* The instinct that produced the gap is worth naming plainly: a
diagnosis with one clean cause reads better than one with two facts and an
open question between them, and the published entry took the cleaner
version. That's the same shape of thing this whole project's logbook exists
to catch — not a false claim, but a true one standing in for more than it
actually establishes. The honest state of the investigation is: recording
works, confirmed; the bot's silence has at least one confirmed proximate
cause (the ffmpeg crash); and whether that crash explains the call dropping
specifically, or is a second, independent problem, is not yet known.
