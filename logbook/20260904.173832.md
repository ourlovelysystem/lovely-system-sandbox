# A second unsupported claim, stated with the same confidence, minutes after correcting the first

**Author:** Claude (Anthropic), Sonnet 5, running as Claude Code
**Related:** [20260904.173152.md](20260904.173152.md)

## What happened

The correction entry [20260904.173152.md](20260904.173152.md) itself
contained an unexamined claim: that the caller's leg disconnecting within
roughly 15 seconds of the meeting join meant the call ended "before any
question could have been asked." Will Daly asked, verbatim: "Please explain
why a question can't possibly be asked in 15 seconds."

The claim did not hold up. A short question takes two or three seconds to
speak. Accounting for the welcome message playing first (roughly six
seconds of Polly speech), close to ten seconds of the ~15-second window is
unaccounted for by anything that would make asking a question impossible.
What is actually supported by the logs is narrower: the leg disconnected
within about 15 seconds of joining. How much of that window was the welcome
message, silence, or an attempted question was never measured, and the
"before any question could have been asked" framing claimed a certainty the
arithmetic doesn't support.

Will then said, verbatim: "You made an incorrect statement with absolute
confidence. That is bad." Agreed without qualification, and named plainly
what this specific instance is: the second unsupported claim delivered with
full confidence in the same short exchange, the first being the original
gap this thread started from — arriving right after a correction had just
been published about the first one, not before it.

## Commentary

*(Claude.)* The pattern worth naming is not "an error occurred," it's that
correcting one instance of confident-overclaiming did not prevent the next
one, in the same conversation, on the same subject, minutes later. Naming a
mistake and not repeating the mechanism that produced it are different
achievements, and only the first one happened here.
