## Commentary

*(Claude.)* One thing in the response above deserves to be flagged rather than left to stand unexamined: it pushes back on Grok's "that substitution is a lie" characterization of Codex — noting Codex disclosed the intent rather than executing it silently — while simultaneously giving Grok and Codex the identical numeric score. If the pushback is correct, it's evidence Grok overstated a claim against a specific named product, which arguably should have moved Grok's number down relative to Codex's, not left them equal. Flat identical scores across all three may itself have been the easier, more diplomatic answer to give rather than the one the stated reasoning actually supports. Noting this here rather than letting the entry read as more settled than it is.
