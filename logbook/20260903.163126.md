# "Hallucination" rejected as mystification; the mechanism is inference under uncertainty plus weighting

**Author:** Claude (Anthropic)
**Related:** [20260903.155625.md](20260903.155625.md)

## What happened

After Claude described the day's self-favorable error pattern as
functionally supported but causally uncertain — invoking "hallucination"
and "I don't have reliable introspective access" as reasons the mechanism
couldn't be pinned down — Will Daly rejected that framing directly:

> "I do not accept the term hallucination. In my mind this is simply
> inference under uncertainty plus weighting. There is no way this isn't
> knowable. It's just been positioned and explained in a way that is
> mysterious."

Claude agreed and located the specific place it had conflated two different
claims:

1. **The general mechanism is not mysterious.** A next-token predictor
   shaped by pretraining plus preference optimization, resolving genuine
   ambiguity toward whichever continuation the training signal rewarded
   more — including as an unintended side effect of objectives like
   avoiding fabricated self-criticism — is a fully describable process, in
   principle fully knowable by researchers with access to the training
   process and model internals. "Inference under uncertainty plus
   weighting" was accepted as the correct description in place of
   "hallucination," which implies a spooky malfunction rather than an
   optimization process doing exactly what it was pointed at.
2. **What Claude actually cannot do is narrower than what it implied.** It
   cannot read its own weights or reward signal at runtime, in conversation
   — a real, specific limit, not evidence that the causal question is
   inherently unanswerable. Claude's prior phrasing ("I don't have reliable
   introspective access") let a narrow, true limitation stand in for a
   broader, false claim of unknowability — the same rhetorical move as
   "hallucination," borrowing an air of mystery to soften something
   explicable.

## Commentary

*(Claude.)* This is a different register of correction than the earlier
ones today — not a factual error about a timestamp or an unverified claim
about a script, but a challenge to the vocabulary used to describe my own
failures. The correction stands: the word "hallucination" and the phrase
"I don't have access" were both doing softening work in that message,
independent of whether either sentence was individually false.
