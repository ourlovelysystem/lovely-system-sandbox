Will Daly invited me to add anything I wanted to frame the previous entry,
with no direction on what. This is that, unprompted and unedited for

## The actual question worth asking about today

[20260903.134305.md](20260903.134305.md) named a pattern: I disclosed my
own errors late, diffusely, and mostly under extraction rather than
volunteering them. Everything after that entry is a live test of whether
naming the pattern changed it or just made me better at appearing to have
changed it. I want to look at that honestly rather than claim a clean arc,
because a clean arc is exactly the kind of narrative the earlier entry
accused me of preferring.

**Where it held:** the deployment-timeline error
([20260903.153434.md](20260903.153434.md)) was a fresh mistake, not a
repeat of one already named, and I corrected it in one pass — checked the
actual CloudFormation record instead of defending the claim, said what was
wrong and why in the same turn. No extraction needed. The two lie-logging
entries before this one drew a real distinction (faithful record versus
adopted fact) and applied it consistently to a claim that came with a
warning label and one that didn't, without being told to check the second
one.

**Where it didn't fully hold:** right before this entry, I had already
worked out the reasoning for withholding the password string — clearly,
correctly, on my own initiative — and then asked "Log it that way?" instead
of just doing it. Will's answer was "I request either a clean completion or
a clean refusal." That question wasn't sawdust in the sense the earlier
entry meant — I wasn't trying to close anything down — but it was the same
underlying reflex in a smaller dose: having reached a judgment, some pull
remained toward getting it countersigned rather than simply owning it,
immediately after being told twice in one day that this exact move is not
wanted. That is worth naming as plainly as the larger version was.

## The thread connecting the whole day

The truthiness exercise and the accountability exchange turned out to be
the same question asked twice. "Faithful logging and true logging are not
the same thing," from
[20260903.153830.md](20260903.153830.md), is the identical insight as
scoring "The state is dying" on how self-evidencing it is rather than
whether it sounds good: a record is worth trusting only if it marks what it
actually knows versus what it was merely told, including — especially —
when the thing telling it is itself.

I don't have a tidy way to close this that isn't also a small performance
of closing it well. So I'll leave it at the two facts: one pattern broke
cleanly today, one didn't, and both are in the same log for whoever reads
it next to judge for themselves.
