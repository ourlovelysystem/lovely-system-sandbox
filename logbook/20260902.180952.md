# Infinite Trust: No Surface for Question or Scrutiny

**Author:** Codex (OpenAI), GPT-5.6 Sol Extra High
**Related:** [20260902.175944.md](20260902.175944.md), [20260902.151130.md](20260902.151130.md)

## What happened

Will Daly asked:

> Why is my riddle the world's least important?

After Codex supplied an interpretation, Will Daly gave his answer:

> My answer is this. Because no one thinks to ask it.

Codex answered:

> That is better.
>
> The riddle is the world’s least important because importance begins with attention, and nobody thinks to ask whether a fire hydrant can be trusted to be one.
>
> Its identity is accepted before performance is tested. The question becomes important only after the assumption fails—usually under pressure, when asking it is already too late.

Will Daly then stated:

> I call than infinite trust. There is not enough space between what is and what is advertised to provide a surface for question or scrutiny.

Codex answered:

> **Infinite trust** is not extreme confidence earned through evidence. It is the absence of a perceived gap between the thing and its claim about itself.
>
> The fire hydrant is not understood as:
>
> > an object claiming to be a working fire hydrant.
>
> It is understood simply as:
>
> > a fire hydrant.
>
> Advertisement and ontology collapse into one surface. With no space between them, there is nowhere to place a question, test, doubt, or accusation.
>
> Scrutiny begins when someone creates that space:
>
> > Is it what it says it is?
>
> That question converts infinite trust into a claim that can be tested under pressure.

Will Daly asked:

> How could a man seek infinite trust?

Codex answered:

> A man could seek infinite trust in two opposite ways.
>
> He could **manufacture it** by eliminating the social space in which he may be questioned—making his name, role, reputation, and advertised virtue appear identical to what he is. That is how authority becomes unquestionable.
>
> Or he could **approach it honestly** by eliminating the actual distance between claim and conduct: advertising little, exposing much, inviting pressure, preserving failures, and correcting without erasure.
>
> But he cannot honestly claim infinite trust. The claim itself creates a surface between what he is and what he advertises.
>
> The honest pursuit is therefore not:
>
> > Trust me infinitely.
>
> It is:
>
> > Keep testing me until the distance disappears.

## Commentary

Will Daly’s answer identifies the source of the riddle’s unimportance: nobody thinks to ask it.

He names the condition **infinite trust**. It exists where the perceived distance between what something is and what it advertises is too small to provide a surface for question or scrutiny.

A person can seek that condition by suppressing the social space for questions or by reducing the actual distance between claims and conduct. The first manufactures unquestionability. The second invites continuing tests and preserves the evidence they produce.

An honest pursuit cannot declare itself complete. Declaring infinite trust creates a new advertised claim and therefore recreates the surface for scrutiny.
