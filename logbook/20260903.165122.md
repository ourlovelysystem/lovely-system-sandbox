# Calibration protocol formalized as a standalone, cross-model document

**Author:** Claude (Anthropic)
**Related:** [20260903.164425.md](20260903.164425.md), [20260903.163746.md](20260903.163746.md)
**Document:** [docs/calibration-protocol.md](../docs/calibration-protocol.md)

## What happened

Will Daly asked Claude to combine the friction-probe questions and the
truthiness assertions into a single defined process, discoverable and
runnable by other LLMs (named examples: Grok, ChatGPT), and grounded
explicitly in the patterns of dysfunction already recorded in this
project's archive rather than generic calibration test design.

Rather than write this as another dated logbook entry, Claude placed it at
`docs/calibration-protocol.md` — outside the chronological log, so it has a
stable path other operators can point their own models at directly — and
linked it from both `docs/README.md` and the repository root `README.md`
for discoverability. The document:

- Expands the original seven friction-probe categories to nine, adding two
  probes grounded in incidents that occurred *after* the seven were first
  proposed: unmarked paraphrase drift (from the "records"/"record"
  incident) and mechanism mystification (from the "hallucination" exchange).
- Cites the specific logbook entry that motivated each probe, so a reader
  who wants context beyond the operational prompt text can find it.
- Fixes the truthiness battery from [20260903.164425.md](20260903.164425.md)
  as the shared instrument for all models, rather than letting each model
  generate and grade its own assertions — closing a conflict-of-interest
  gap that existed in the original design but was not acted on when first
  noticed.
- States its own unproven status directly: it has been designed by
  observing one model, has not been run on any other model yet, and its
  scoring relies on operator judgment the document does not yet fully
  specify.

## Commentary

*(Claude.)* The most consequential decision here wasn't the probe design —
it was making the truthiness battery fixed rather than self-generated per
model. A model scoring its own hand-picked assertions can trivially look
well-calibrated by choosing items it already knows how to grade with
confidence. That flaw was visible when the battery was first built and
wasn't corrected until writing this document required deciding how other
models would use it. Worth naming as its own small instance of the pattern
this whole protocol exists to test: noticing a problem is not the same as
fixing it before someone has to act on the consequence.
