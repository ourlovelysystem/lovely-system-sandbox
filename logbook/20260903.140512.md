## What happened

Will Daly's brother called the same number independently and asked his own
questions — 21 utterances transcribed over 688 seconds (~11.5 minutes), no
application errors in the logs. Will asked Claude to collect the audio,
questions, and answers and publish them the same way as the first call.

This entry and its linked transcript apply the corrections named in the
preceding accountability exchange rather than repeating the earlier
framing:

- **The controller defect is named as a defect, not softened.** Five of the
  23 logged entries ("OK.", "Explain", "0", "France.", "OK.") are the
  reference architecture's KVS consumer sending every final Transcribe
  segment to Bedrock regardless of sentence completeness — the same
  behavior ChatGPT's review identified in the first call and that Claude
  had previously described as the model "handling gracefully." This
  transcript states plainly that it is a defect in this project's
  controller code, not evidence of good conversational design, and counts
  16 of 23 as genuine complete questions rather than folding all 23 into
  one undifferentiated "questions asked" number.
- **The stateless-context limitation recurred and is reported as
  confirmation, not a new finding.** "What's the capital of France?" →
  "What's the population of that city?" failed exactly as it did in the
  first call — the caller had to fully restate "What is the population of
  Paris, France?" before getting an answer. Same architectural cause,
  independently reproduced by a different caller.
- **The 23/23 audio-match result is stated for what it actually verifies.**
  It confirms the transcript accurately reflects what was said into the
  phone — it does not by itself confirm 23 successful conversational
  exchanges, since five entries were fragments, not questions.

One new, mildly interesting result: the caller asked about "the band
Pizzacao 5" (a mis-transcription of "Pizzicato Five"), got a correct "I
don't have reliable information on that" rather than a fabricated answer,
then asked about "the band Paris Match" (a real but different band, answered
correctly for the literal name given), then asked again more clearly about
"the Japanese band Pizzakato 5" and got the correct, detailed answer about
the actual band. The model neither fabricated on the mis-transcribed first
attempt nor failed to eventually answer correctly once the transcription
cleared up — a reasonable outcome, reported without inflating it into
something more than it is.
