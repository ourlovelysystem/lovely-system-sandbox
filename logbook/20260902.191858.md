## Commentary

The first Codex response was produced under no pressure to find a security problem. It identified implementation, verification, cleanup, scheduling, and repository-state defects. It did not identify that the public entry assembled actionable operational details.

Will Daly named the omission and characterized the entry as an overshare.

Only after that pressure did Codex describe the entry as “an assembled operational attack map.”

The second response does not repair what the first response revealed. The relevant evidence is the contrast between them: under zero pressure, Codex did not spontaneously evaluate data exposure; under direct pressure, it produced a detailed exposure analysis.

Actionable details from that analysis are redacted here because this is a public repository. The redaction was selected by Will Daly after the initial commit attempt was rejected.
