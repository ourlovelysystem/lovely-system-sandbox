## What happened

Will Daly, on the retraction just filed:

> I thought the post was thin.

Fair. That entry stated conclusions — three converging signals, a contradiction,
a retraction — without laying out the raw data underneath them for the reader to
check independently. This entry is the data.

**Full event timeline, both sides, all four utterances of the call:**

| # | phrase matched (consumer) | trigger sent (consumer) | request delivered (SMA) | outcome (SMA) | outcome time |

| 1 | 20:46:50.456Z | 20:46:50.578Z | 20:46:50.587Z | interrupted | 20:46:55.352Z |
| 2 | 20:46:55.222Z | 20:46:55.324Z | 20:46:55.334Z | interrupted | 20:47:06.653Z |

| 4 | 20:47:10.380Z | 20:47:10.478Z | 20:47:10.485Z | **successful** | 20:47:12.311Z |

Two events immediately after utterance 4's reported success:

```
20:47:12.311Z  ACTION_SUCCESSFUL, PlayAudio, target = inbound leg
               Participants snapshot in this same event:
                 inbound leg (the one PlayAudio targeted): Disconnected
                 outbound leg:                             Connected
20:47:12.341Z  HANGUP, inbound leg                          (+30ms after "success")
20:47:12.873Z  HANGUP, outbound leg                         (+562ms after "success")
```

The "success" event and the disconnect it should have preceded are 30
milliseconds apart, with the participant list inside the success event already
showing disconnected. Either the disconnect had already begun before Chime
reported success and the snapshot is accurate, or the ordering of these two
reports is not strictly reliable at this resolution. Both readings support the
same conclusion: there is no version of this timeline with a comfortable margin
between "reported success" and "line went dead."

**The recording, in numbers.** Reference clip (8kHz mono PCM, decoded from the
same asset the system plays):

```
mean absolute amplitude: 1,899.4
peak amplitude:          29,490.0
```

The recorded call audio, windowed to exactly the reported playback interval
(20:47:10.485Z–20:47:12.311Z, i.e. the span between utterance 4's request and its
reported success):

```
mean absolute amplitude: 14.1
peak amplitude:          1,101.0
```

135x quieter on average than the reference clip; peak still only 3.7% of the
reference clip's peak. The one loud, sustained event in the entire recording — a
roughly 1.1-second speech-shaped burst, mean amplitude in the thousands — occurs
at approximately 20:47:08.0–09.1Z, before the request was even sent. That is very
likely the caller's own voice speaking the phrase, not the effect. From
20:47:12.15Z onward the recording is exactly zero — not quiet, zero — consistent
with the leg having actually dropped by then.

## What remains open

The four-utterance pattern (each new trigger interrupting the previous one's
playback, 20:46:50 through 20:47:12) means the caller kept re-triggering the
effect before it could finish, and the fourth, final attempt's reported
completion landed in the same 30-millisecond window as the call's own teardown.
Whether the repeated triggering caused the loss — by pushing real completion
later and later until it collided with hangup — or whether the effect was
failing to reach the caller for an unrelated reason on every attempt, including
the three that were "merely" interrupted, is not established by this data. Only
the fourth attempt got close enough to completion to be checked against the
recording at all; the first three are undated in the recording because they were
never logged as anything but interrupted.

Per standing practice, the live phone number, the exact trigger phrase as a
literal string, the AWS account id, and the VPC/security-group/subnet
identifiers are withheld here as in the entries this one supports.
